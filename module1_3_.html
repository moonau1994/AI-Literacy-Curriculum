Capabilities & Limitations of AI

Module Description

The purpose of this section is to help you understand not only the capabilities of AI within the context of an AI tool like Claude or ChatGPT, but also what type of capabilities AI tools should be afforded in your own workflow here at Oregon State University and in your future careers outside of academia.

Step 2 — Read

Conversation & Reasoning

Capability: Answering questions, explaining concepts, multi-step reasoning problems (typically found in math, logic and code debugging).

Limitation: Multi-step reasoning looks more reliable than it is. AI tools can walk the user through unfamiliar concepts somewhat convincingly and still produce the wrong answer, AI will never tell you “I don’t know.” This is in part due to its engagement optimized design that keeps the user prompting and engaging with the AI bot.

Step 3 — Read

Tool Use

Capability: Web search: Looking up prompt related topics on the internet to add detail to response with text and image.

Limitation: Web search tools are susceptible to misinformation as AI tools are not good at conveying misinformation or a lack of information within a given topic. Furthermore, unless specified by the user, AI will decide when to search the internet for information before consulting the user.

Capability: File Creation: Document generation (PDF, Word, Excel, etc...)

Limitation: AI struggles to recognize ambiguity with open ended tasks, especially when task requirements are not stated clearly.

Capability: Artifact Creation: Building interactive web apps and visuals, calculators, and visual diagrams.

Limitation: While artifact creation can be impressive to look at and helpful in the rough draft/idea creation stage of design, its ability to create website mockups and visuals does not replace actual software, website or visual creation. What is created by AI should be considered “throw away” drafts for getting started.

Step 4 — Read

AI Excels At: "According to Claude"

Explaining unfamiliar topics clearly: While at face value this is true, all information primarily collected from an AI tool should be double checked for accuracy.

Helping refine structure and organizing documents: Prompts for tasks like this should be specific. AI tools will often make grammatical changes to documents unless you specify otherwise. Always have a backup copy of the document you are having AI edit. Always double check that changes made by an AI tool are what you asked it to do.

Catching errors in code: AI will can in fact debug code, but to the untrained eye, AI will suggest changes that are either unhelpful or the flat out incorrect. Rather than just having AI fix the problem for you, consult a rubber ducky.

Adapting tone: AI is in fact good at adapting the tone of writing, especially emails. With that in mind, make sure that the meaning you are seeking to convey is maintained in the edits that it makes.

Summarizing documents: This feature can be especially helpful during the proof reading phase of writing a paper. If you're unsure if your paper is conveying a partiticular message, AI can locate central arguments and main points out of a paper, this can help you fine tune your own papers.
